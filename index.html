Daily Formation Synthesis.

What is the Church teaching your soul today?

Today the Church calls you to lay down the case you could rightfully make. You have standing — as a son, as a believer with real gifts and real claims — but the readings put before you a Savior who owned the Temple and paid its tax anyway, and an apostle who could have commanded prayer but begged for it instead. Greatness in this kingdom is not what you can prove is owed to you; it is how far down you are willing to go without needing anyone to notice. Become like the child in the midst of them: small, unguarded, without a case to argue.

Ascetic posture for today: When you feel the urge to assert what is technically yours to claim, pause and ask what humility would choose instead.

From the Orthodox Study Bible

Matthew 17:24-27The temple tax was an annual head tax on all male Jews (except the priests) over twelve years of age for the maintenance of the temple (see Nm 3:43–51). Since Jesus is the Son of God, He is both High Priest and “proprietor” of the temple, and thus is exempt from the tax. Nevertheless, He pays it anyway, both to avoid unnecessary offense and to show that He has totally identified Himself with mankind.
Matthew 18:1-4This question indicates a selfish interest in worldly power. Pointing to a little child as the model of true discipleship, Jesus emphasizes the virtues required for entrance into the kingdom of heaven: humility, dependence, lowliness, simplicity, obedience, and a willingness to love and be loved. In Orthodox iconography, St. Ignatius of Antioch is depicted as this child. In certain legends of saints, he is the boy who gave the loaves and fishes (Jn 6:9).
