Daily Formation Synthesis.

What is the Church teaching your soul today?

Today the Church calls you back to your own baptism — not as a past event to remember fondly, but as a claim still standing on your life. You went down into water as Christ went down into the Jordan, and the same voice that named Him beloved before He had done anything is the voice spoken over you. That belovedness is not a suspension of testing; it is what makes testing survivable, and it is what will be asked about at the judgment seat — not whether you were tested, but whether you lived, in the test, as someone who already belonged to Him rather than to yourself.

Ascetic posture for today: Meet whatever tests you today already carrying the identity spoken at your baptism, rather than trying to earn it through how the test goes.

From the Orthodox Study Bible

2 Corinthians 5:10Following his discourse on suffering, death, and resurrection (4:16–5:8), Paul now turns to the judgment. We must all appear, not only the unbelievers, to be judged according to what we have done. For Christians, right belief gives power and motivation for right behavior. Here again, the NT unifies faith and works.
2 Corinthians 5:11-15In his gospel, Paul seeks to persuade men (v. 11) on two fronts: (1) by the terror of the Lord (v. 11), the possibility of judgment to eternal death; and (2) by the love of Christ (v. 14), the divine invitation to live for Christ (v. 15), to eternal life. The heart of Paul’s gospel is baptism, where life and death meet: Christ died and rose for us that we might die and rise in Him.
Mark 1:14Mark’s written emphasis on John being put in prison before Jesus begins preaching reveals that a key purpose of the old covenant—to prepare the people for Christ—had been completed (Gal 4:1–5). Once Christ came, the time of preparation was fulfilled. To repent is to do a total “about-face.” The word in Greek literally means to “change one’s mind.” Repentance is a radical change of one’s spirit, mind, thought, and heart—a complete reorientation to a life centered in Christ.
