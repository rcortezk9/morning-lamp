Daily Formation Synthesis.

What is the Church teaching your soul today?

Today the Church calls you to lay down Martha's anxious busyness and take up Mary's stillness — not the stillness of idleness, but the gathered attention of a soul that has made room for the Word to dwell. The Theotokos is not honored today because she achieved something you cannot; she is honored because she received something you also are offered — the same Word, the same invitation to be built into Wisdom's house, the same call to hear and to keep. Her falling asleep is not an ending but a passage, the pattern of self-emptying followed by exaltation that Christ Himself walked first and that she, having walked it after Him in perfect obedience, now walks into glory. The gate that was shut for every other is open to you too, not by force but by welcome — if, like her, you will hear the word of God and keep it.

Ascetic posture for today: Choose, at least once today, Mary's stillness over Martha's anxious motion — stop, listen, and let the word of God land before you rush to serve it.

From the Orthodox Study Bible

Philippians 2:5-11This passage is a hymn already in use in the Church, quoted here by St. Paul because it calls us to ponder the humility of Christ, a truth necessary for suffering Christians to understand and live out. The passage has been incorporated into many hymns of the Orthodox Church. Christ is He who, being in the form of God, is also in the form of a bondservant (v. 7), in our likeness (see Gn 1:26), and is voluntarily subject to death. He who has died, God has exalted and given the name Lord (v. 11), a name God the Son shares with God the Father from all eternity.
Philippians 2:6The form (Gr. morphe) of God, a concept parallel with “the image [Gr. eikon] of God” (Col 1:15), refers to the Son’s sharing in full the divine nature. Robbery (Gr. harpagmon, lit. “prize” or “booty”) refers to an object stolen and tightly clutched. Christ has equality with God not by seizure but by nature, and with absolute security. There is, therefore, no threat, loss, or any change in the divine nature of the Son of God when He takes our humanity to Himself and offers us salvation.
Philippians 2:7Made Himself of no reputation (v. 7; lit. “emptied Himself”) deals with the Son’s will, not His nature. He emptied Himself not by laying down His divine nature or setting it aside, but by voluntarily taking on our human nature. To human beings He looks just like another human being, for being truly incarnate, He is fully man by nature. He took the form of a bondservant, voluntarily sharing our human condition except for one thing: sin. In His humanity, He showed the fullness of humility by His obedience to the death that has enslaved humanity. To die on a cross, the death of a criminal, was repulsive to the Romans and considered a curse by the Jews. But His death brings life to all who are joined to Him.
Luke 10:38-42Mary and Martha are the sisters of Lazarus, whom Jesus raised from the dead (Jn 11:1). Martha is not rebuked for serving, but for complaining and for being distracted, worried, and troubled. In following Christ, we serve in order to facilitate the spread of the gospel (see Acts 6:1–4).
Luke 11:27These verses are read on most feasts of the Virgin Mary. Jesus corrects the woman from the crowd, not by denouncing his mother, but by emphasizing her faith. People are blessed in God’s eyes if, like Mary, they hear the word of God and keep it. The Greek word menounge, here rendered more than that, is translated “Yes indeed” in Rom 10:18. This word corrects by amplifying, not by negating.
