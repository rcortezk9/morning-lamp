Daily Formation Synthesis.

What is the Church teaching your soul today?

Today the Church calls you to stop measuring your worth by your activity and start measuring it by your attention. Christ emptied Himself all the way to a cross before He was raised — that is the shape of every true blessedness the readings name today, whether it is Mary's stillness, the hidden wisdom of the cross, the cost of discipleship, or the discipline that saves a sinner's soul, or the endurance that outlasts collapsing empires and temples. None of it is won by noise, status, or self-assurance. All of it is received by those willing to sit still enough to hear, humble enough to be emptied, and steady enough to endure. Sit at His feet today before you rush to serve Him.

Ascetic posture for today: Before your next task, pause for one unhurried minute of silence with a single line from today's Gospel, refusing to let busyness stand in for listening.

From the Orthodox Study Bible

1 Corinthians 4:18-20Paul will soon visit Corinth and face those who prejudge him to see if any power (Gr. dunamis) lies behind their word. Citizenship in the kingdom of God, discovered in the Church, does not consist in mere talk, but is manifested in a growing life of grace.
1 Corinthians 5:1Sexual immorality (Gr. porneia, from which we get “pornography”) originally meant prostitution, later immorality in general. In this case of incest, his father’s wife is not the man’s own mother, but a stepmother. Even so, this sin is grievous among Jews and Gentiles alike.
1 Corinthians 5:2The Greek word for mourned (pentheo) is also used in the Septuagint to refer to (1) grieving for the dead, and (2) sorrow over unrepentant sinners (see Neh 8:9; Is 24:4; Dan 10:2; Am 8:8). Taken away from among you refers to excommunication.
1 Corinthians 5:4My spirit (Gr. pneuma) is Paul’s human spirit, which is being sanctified by the Holy Spirit.
1 Corinthians 5:5In the Church there is protection from the destructive power of Satan (Mt 16:18). This protection is here removed through excommunication in the hope that, having experienced the difference between God’s rule and Satan’s, this offender may repent and in the end be saved. The flesh (Gr. sarx) is not synonymous with “body” (Gr. soma; see v. 3). Paul is calling for the destruction not of the body but rather of the lusts of the flesh.
Matthew 24:1Christ’s prophecy of the destruction of the temple was fulfilled in AD 70, when the temple was destroyed by the Romans (see note at 22:7).
Matthew 24:3-31The Scriptures describe the end times in a variety of ways, so that no precise chronology can be determined (see Dan 7–12; Mk 13; Lk 21; 1Co 15:51–55; 1Th 4:13–17; 2Th 2:1–10; and the Book of Revelation). The Lord’s emphasis is on watchfulness and the practice of virtue rather than on constructing timetables of things that have not yet happened. In Matthew’s account here, the end is described as encompassing (1) the initial sorrows (vv. 4–14), (2) the great tribulation (vv. 15–28), and (3) the coming of the Son of Man (vv. 29–31). The period of the great tribulation includes the entire Christian era and is not limited to the final years before Christ’s return.
Matthew 24:4The warnings against deception are given the most emphasis. Of particular importance is the warning against following a false Christ, which Jesus stresses again in vv. 11, 23–27.
Matthew 24:6The wars here refer first and foremost to the Roman destruction of Jerusalem, but certainly include subsequent wars. Wars are not a sign of the imminent end, but of the opposite—that the end is not yet (see 1Th 5:1–3).
